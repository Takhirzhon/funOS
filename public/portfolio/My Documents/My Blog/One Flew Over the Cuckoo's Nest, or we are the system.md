# One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest, or we are the system

![One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest](/portfolio/My%20Documents/My%20Blog/Pictures/One%20Flew%20Over%20the%20Cuckoo's%20Nest,%20poster.jpg)

Can one person change the system? Can one person help a society free itself from a System that took years to build? Despite the quizzes and the algorithms deadlines, I decided I had to watch *One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest*. I do not regret it. I came out with a pile of emotions and a pile of questions.

Spoilers. You have been warned.

The plot, briefly: a man tries to help the people in a psychiatric hospital get past their problems, and at the same time fights the head nurse, who has made a schedule and will not change it - and who, more than that, constantly humiliates and leans on sick people, because she knows exactly where each of them is weak (this one has trouble with girls, this one with his wife, this one with his parents; and some are simply beyond reason, vegetative). McMurphy stands against the nurse and against the injustice done inside those walls.

While watching I kept asking why the film is called what it is called. By the end it hit me. Everyone knows the cuckoo lays her eggs in other birds' nests. Here the author has decided that the hospital is the cuckoo's nest, the chicks are the patients - to whom the cuckoo is relentless and heartless - and the hero is an ordinary bird who wants to be let go.

And I thought: we all live in a system. And the system is not what surrounds us. We *are* the system; it exists because of us. Not everyone can push back, and if you do, what waits for you is the "electroshock" most people get - or the highest measure, a lobotomy, after which you are no longer a threat. Yes, we are in a system. A system inside a system: first the family, then the neighbourhood, then school, university, the state.

*"So when are you going to study?"*

*"Why aren't you married yet?"*

*"When are the children coming?"*

I think everyone has heard at least one of these. The book shows, in colour, that people who live inside the system are easier to manage. I asked a lot of questions: what does McMurphy want out of all this? Is he crazy or not? What exactly is he after - friendship, fame, money?

No. We want out of the system. But if we get out, what is the price? Our life, like McMurphy's? Or will they let us fly?

## Postscript

More fuel for this came from an argument with one of my professors, about the state and politics. Her position: our vote decides nothing, forget it, we are in the system. Fighting changes nothing; you will always be under someone, and you will not even know who, however high you climb.

I tried to argue. Then she brought up the value of a life - that to the state we are a resource (geography has the term: *human resource*, the economic use you are to the country). I remembered a video about the value of a human life and just agreed with her: to the system we are a sum of money, and the earlier we die - at 35 or 40, say - the more of it the state keeps.

So, Machiavelli's *The Prince* next. I think I will learn a lot. People I argue with often ask why a software guy is so interested in politics. Bismarck (borrowing from Pericles) had the answer: *if you do not take an interest in politics, politics will take an interest in you.*
