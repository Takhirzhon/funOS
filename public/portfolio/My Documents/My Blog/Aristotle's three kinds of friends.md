# Aristotle's three kinds of friends

![Aristotle, Palazzo Altemps](/portfolio/My%20Documents/My%20Blog/Pictures/Aristotle,%20Palazzo%20Altemps.jpg)

How do you tell a real friend?

Start with the uncomfortable truth: half of the people you count as friends do not count you as one.

Aristotle sorted friendship into three kinds, in the *Nicomachean Ethics*, and I lean toward his sorting.

**1. Friendship of utility.** You know how to keep livestock and the other person knows how to run a business. This friendship ends when the use ends. You are not looking at the person; you are looking at what the person can give.

**2. Friendship of pleasure.** You go to clubs together, to the cinema, you argue about football. It runs on emotion, it is fickle, and it is not aimed at the person or his inner life either. It does not matter who is next to you, as long as it is fun and comfortable. This kind is unstable and falls apart as soon as the source of the pleasure goes away. I have had a whole circle of friends that, looking at it honestly, was this.

**3. Friendship of virtue.** Here you are interested in the person himself. Not what he can bring, not whether he is fun - the first question is whether this is a worthy person, whether there is someone in there. It grows between people who admire each other for what is inside: fairness, honesty, wisdom, kindness. Each wants good for the other not for his own benefit but because it is right.

Aristotle: *"A friend is one soul living in two bodies. A friend to all is a friend to none."*

Most of my confusion about friendship went away once I stopped expecting the third kind from people who had only ever offered the second. That is not an insult to them. It is just the sorting.
